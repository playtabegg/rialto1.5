Windows keeps a list of publishers it trusts. Signing puts your studio on it, so the install prompt says who made the game instead of showing a yellow "Unknown Publisher" warning. It costs about $10 a month, you set it up once, and after that every disc you make signs itself.

You do not need this. Bringing your own signing account is optional, unsigned discs work perfectly, and plenty of people ship them. It is the difference between a disc that looks homemade and one that looks like it came from a studio.

Without a signature
Unknown Publisher
A yellow shield. Antivirus treats every new build as a stranger. Windows 11 machines with Smart App Control can refuse it outright.
With yours
Your Studio, LLC
Your verified name. Reputation builds against your certificate across every release you ever ship, and tampering breaks the signature.
One honest note about discs The big SmartScreen popup only fires on files downloaded from the internet. A file on a disc physically cannot carry the download marker, so disc players never see it, signed or not. On a disc, signing is about the publisher's name and about antivirus. If you also sell downloads, it matters a great deal more.

What Rialto signs01

setup.exe

Your installer. The file players actually run, and the one that shows the publisher name on the Windows prompt.

menu\menu.exe

This disc's own copy of the disc menu.

That second one is deliberate and worth thirty seconds. Rialto is open source, and the menu.exe shipped in its releases is the same generic file on every disc that anybody builds. If it arrived pre-signed, some other studio's name would be sitting on your disc, and your name would be sitting on discs made by people you have never met. So the public copy ships unsigned on purpose, and Rialto signs your copy, on your disc, with your account. The shared original is never touched.

If signing is not set up Your staged menu.exe simply stays unsigned and the build carries on. That is the normal do-it-yourself result and there is nothing wrong with it.

The setup, once03

Assume you have signed nothing before. Follow these in order. Steps 1 through 7 happen once in your life.

The whole thing in three steps One: open an Azure account, register the Microsoft.CodeSigning provider on it, and make an Artifact Signing account on the Basic plan. Two: prove who you are, and when that passes, make a Public Trust certificate profile and give yourself the signer role. Three: install the Azure CLI, run az login, and put three values into Rialto. Step two is the long one, at 1 to 20 business days. The nine steps below are those same three, one click at a time.
1

An Azure account with a real subscription

Sign up at portal.azure.com and put a pay-as-you-go subscription on it. A free trial subscription will not work with this service. You are not charged for signing yet, only for what you use.

2

Turn the service on for that subscription

Search the portal for Subscriptions, pick yours, open Resource providers in the left menu, find Microsoft.CodeSigning and click Register. It will say NotRegistered until you do. This is the step people skip and then cannot work out why nothing appears.

3

Create the signing account

Search the portal for Artifact Signing Accounts and click Create. Make a new resource group, give the account a name (3 to 24 letters and numbers, globally unique, starts with a letter), pick a region near you, and choose the Basic plan: $9.99 a month for 5,000 signatures.

Write the region down. Rialto asks for it, and an account in one region with an endpoint from another fails with a 403 hours later, when everything looks correct.

4

Prove who you are

Inside the new account, open Identity validations and click New identity, then Public. You need the Artifact Signing Identity Verifier role for the button to be clickable.

As an individual: your details are pulled from the billing account on the subscription, so make sure the legal name and address there are exactly what you want printed on the certificate. You verify with a government photo ID and a phone camera.

As a company: you enter the legal entity, a website on a domain you own, two monitored email addresses on that domain, a business identifier and the business address. A named individual at the company still does the photo ID step.

The name you enter here is the name players will see. Enter it exactly.

1 to 20 business days
5

Create a certificate profile

Once validation says Completed: Certificate profiles, Create, type Public Trust. Name it something you will recognise, leave Program Type on None, and pick your validated identity under Verified CN and O. Write the profile name down too, Rialto asks for it.

6

Give yourself permission to sign

On the signing account, open Access control (IAM) and give your own user the role Artifact Signing Certificate Profile Signer. Creating the account does not grant it. Without it every screen looks correct and signing fails at the last second.

7

Two free installs, then sign in

The Azure CLI from aka.ms/azure-cli, and the .NET 8 runtime from dotnet.microsoft.com/download/dotnet/8.0. You also want the Windows SDK for signtool, which you probably already have if you have oscdimg for building ISOs.

Then open a terminal and run az login, once. A browser window appears; sign in with the same account. You will not have to do this again for a long time.

8

Tell Rialto, and check

Click Configure Signing. Choose your region (which fills in the endpoint), paste the account name and the certificate profile name exactly as the portal spells them, and click Save.

Then click Check My Setup. It tests every item above, one line each, and marks anything still to do with what to do about it. Press it again until it says You are ready to sign.

9

Build

Tick Sign Final Build, press Build Game Installer and walk away. Rialto compiles the installer, signs it, signs this disc's copy of menu.exe, verifies the signature and packs the ISO in one go. Your .msix exports get signed too.

On the first signed build only, Rialto downloads Microsoft's signing add-on, about 20 MB, by itself. Nothing you sign ever leaves your PC: only a fingerprint of the file goes to Microsoft, and the signature comes back.

Rather be walked through it?04

Copy the prompt below and paste it into a desktop AI assistant: Claude, ChatGPT, Grok, Kimi or similar. It takes you through the Azure portal one step at a time, waits for you at each one, and finishes by handing you the three values Rialto asks for. Nothing about your game is in it.

Paste this into your assistant 
I am an indie game developer on Windows. I want to code-sign my game's installer with Azure Artifact Signing (Microsoft renamed it from Azure Trusted Signing in January 2026 - the portal now says "Artifact Signing Accounts"). I have never signed anything before and I am not an Azure person.

Walk me through it one step at a time, waiting for me to say done before moving on. Assume I know nothing about Azure. Tell me exactly what to click.

Cover, in this order:
1. Creating an Azure account with a pay-as-you-go subscription (a free trial subscription does not work for this service).
2. Registering the Microsoft.CodeSigning resource provider on that subscription.
3. Creating an Artifact Signing account on the Basic plan (about $9.99/month, 5,000 signatures), and how to pick the right region.
4. Identity validation. I am an INDIVIDUAL developer, not a company - tell me what ID I need, warn me about the parts people get wrong, and tell me how long it usually takes. If I say I am a company instead, switch to the organisation path.
5. Creating a Public Trust certificate profile once validation passes.
6. Giving my own user the "Artifact Signing Certificate Profile Signer" role, and explaining why signing fails without it.
7. Installing the Azure CLI and running az login.
8. Finding the three values I have to type into my build tool: the region endpoint URL, the account name, and the certificate profile name. Tell me exactly where in the portal each one is.

At the end, print those three values back to me as a labelled list so I can copy them across. If a step fails, help me diagnose it before we move on.

The same prompt sits inside Rialto, under Configure Signing, with a copy button next to it.

When it goes wrong06

What you seeWhat it means
403 Forbidden, or SignerSign() failedAlmost always the region. Your endpoint has to match the region the account and the certificate profile live in. Pick the region from Rialto's dropdown rather than typing a URL.
Authentication or credential errorsRun az login again. The sign-in does expire eventually.
Everything looks right, signing still refusedThe Artifact Signing Certificate Profile Signer role, step 6. Creating the account does not grant it to you.
signtool.exe not foundInstall the Windows SDK from aka.ms/windowssdk. Rialto needs the version that supports /dlib.
Something about .NETThe signing add-on needs the .NET 8 runtime. Check My Setup says which version it found.
Identity validation failedStart a new request. Common causes: the billing account's legal name not matching your ID, an email address that rejects links from outside senders, or supporting documents older than twelve months.
Timing, if you sign by hand Rialto signs the staged menu.exe before it compiles the installer, so with automatic signing on, both the copy on the disc and the copy inside setup.exe carry your signature. At the manual pause the installer has already been compiled, so signing menu\menu.exe there covers the copy on the disc, not the copy the installer lays down. Sign it anyway if you like: it is in the same build folder as setup.exe.
Bring your own account, always Rialto is a tool, not a signing service. Everyone who uses it signs with their own Microsoft account and their own name. Your signing identity never touches anyone else's builds, and nobody else's touches yours.
